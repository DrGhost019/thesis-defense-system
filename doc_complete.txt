1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose of the document
This document records the design of the Thesis Defense Scheduling System in its second version. It explains what
the system must do, which data it stores, and how that data moves between people, processes and storage. It is
written so that a developer can build the back-end and both client applications from it, and so that a supervisor can
check each requirement against a precise description. Every design element comes with a short rationale, so the
reasons behind each decision remain visible.

1.2 Background and problem statement
At present a Master's or PhD student who is ready to defend a thesis must find a date that suits every professor
involved. This normally happens through phone calls, messages and visits to offices. The effort is repeated for
each professor, information about who is free is scattered, and defenses are sometimes missed because a
participant forgets the date. The university wants one platform, available on the web and on Android, that publishes
when professors and committees are available, lets students request a defense in a few steps, lets the
administration decide who and when, and reminds everybody before the defense.

1.3 Scope
In scope. Account management and login for all roles; definition of availability by professors and by committees,
as single or recurring slots; a weekly schedule grid that can be viewed and downloaded as PDF; defense requests
submitted by students; assignment of each request to a slot and professor or committee by the administrator;
approval or rejection by the assigned professors; cancellation; messaging inside confirmed defenses; and
notifications through a Bale bot.
Out of scope. Grading, thesis document storage, room booking, payments, and any notification channel other
than the in-app notices and the Bale bot. Rescheduling is not a separate function: it is a cancellation followed by a
new request.

1.5 Objectives
• One shared view of availability: every professor and committee publishes time once, and everyone sees it in
one weekly grid.

• Simple student requests: a student submits a request without knowing or contacting any professor.
• Organizational control: the administrator, who knows workloads and committee rules, decides the slot and the
professor or committee.

• Fewer missed defenses: a reminder reaches each participant one day before the defense, through the
messenger they actually use.

• Transparency and traceability: every request keeps a status and a history of who decided what and when.
• One back-end for two clients: the web and Android applications use the same data model and rules.

1.7 Design decisions
Decision

Description and reason

Committee-based defenses

A defense can involve several professors. One DefenseSession has one DefenseParticipant per
professor, and the session is confirmed only when all participants approve.

Admin-assigned scheduling

Only the administrator links a request to a slot. Students never see a professor list for choosing,
which removes the negotiation the brief wants to avoid.

Occupancy belongs to a date, not
to a slot

A recurring slot repeats, so it cannot be "reserved" as a whole. The booking is stored on the
session as a concrete date; the slot stays available for its other dates.

Grid is derived data

The weekly grid is computed from slots on demand, so it is always current and needs no storage
or synchronization.

Notifications go through one log

Every Bale message is recorded in a single Notification table, which allows retries, prevents
duplicates and supports troubleshooting.

In-app notices remain

Notices inside the application (new request, assignment, cancellation) are unchanged. Only the
outcome and the reminder travel by Bale.

Simple cancellation

Either the student or an assigned professor can cancel; rescheduling is a cancellation followed by
a new request.

2.4 Constraints and assumptions
• Platforms: web application and Android application on one shared back-end. The web application is built by
the author; the Android application by a project partner.

• Notification channel: only Bale (and in-app notices). Email is not used for notifications.
• Calendar: the working week is Saturday to Wednesday, 08:00 to 17:00. Odd days are Saturday, Monday and
Wednesday; even days are Sunday and Tuesday (confirmed).

• Approval: professors approve or reject after the administrator assigns the defense (confirmed).
• Reminder timing: the daily reminder run happens once per day in the morning (assumed 09:00 Tehran time)
and covers defenses dated the next day.

• Single university: one institution, one administrator group; multi-tenant operation is not required.
• Internship setting: the project is delivered in a limited period by one web developer, so the design favors
simple, proven techniques.

3. System Architecture
3.1 Architectural overview
The system follows a classic three-tier structure with one shared back-end. The web client and the Android client
are thin: they show data and send commands, and every business rule (validation, assignment checks,
confirmation, notification) is implemented once in the back-end. This guarantees that both clients behave
identically and keeps the Android work independent of the web work, since both depend only on the API contract in
section 8.

3.5 Cross-cutting design principles
• One rule, one place: business rules are in services; clients never decide whether an action is allowed.
• The database is the last line of defense: unique indexes and check constraints reject invalid data even if a
service has a bug.

• Derived data is not stored: the weekly grid and the occupancy of a recurring slot are computed from slots and
sessions.

• Failures are isolated: a Bale failure marks a notification as failed; it never rolls back a scheduling decision.
• Every change is attributable: assignment, answers and cancellations store who and when.
• Idempotent jobs: the daily job can be run twice without sending duplicates, thanks to the unique notification
key.

4. Entity-Relationship Design
4.1 Overview
The data model has ten entities. User holds every account; Student and Professor extend it with role-specific
details. Committee and CommitteeMember describe standing groups of professors. AvailabilitySlot records
published time, owned by one professor or one committee. DefenseSession records one student's defense request
through its whole life, DefenseParticipant records each professor's decision on it, Message holds the conversation,
and Notification logs each Bale message. Keys are surrogate integers; foreign keys use the suffix _id.

4.5.1 Explanation of each relationship
R1 and R2, User with Student and Professor. An account has no profile or exactly one of each kind, and every
profile belongs to exactly one account. The minimum of zero exists because administrators have neither profile,
and because a student has no professor profile and the reverse. A student and a professor profile never exist for
the same account.
R3 and R4, owners of slots. A professor or a committee may have published no slots or many, and every slot has
exactly one owner. Because the owner is either a professor or a committee, both foreign keys are nullable and a
CHECK constraint requires exactly one to be filled. This keeps one slot table while letting the grid show either
name.
R5 and R6, committee membership. A committee has at least one member and many are normal; a professor
may belong to no committee or several. The two relationships together resolve the many-to-many link between
Professor and Committee through CommitteeMember, whose pair (committee_id, professor_id) is unique.
R7, Student requests DefenseSession. A student has none before the first request and may have several over
time, for example after a rejection or cancellation. Every session belongs to exactly one student. At most one
session per student may be active (requested, assigned or confirmed) at a time.
R8, slot assigned to sessions. Over time a slot can serve many sessions: a recurring slot repeats, and even a
single slot can be reused after a cancellation. A session has a slot only after assignment, so the foreign key is
nullable. The concrete date is stored on the session, and (slot_id, scheduled_date) must be unique among active
sessions, which prevents double booking of the same occurrence.

Thesis Defense Scheduling System | Complete Project Documentation (v2)

Page 17

R9, administrator assigns. An administrator may have assigned none or many sessions; a session has at most
one assigning administrator, and none while it is only requested.
R10 and R11, participants. Seats are created at assignment, so a session has no participants before that and
one or more afterwards (one for a professor's slot, one per member for a committee's slot). Every seat belongs to
one session and one professor. Together they resolve the many-to-many link between sessions and professors.
R12 and R13, messages. A session has no messages until it is confirmed and any number afterwards; each
message belongs to one session and has one sender. The sender reference points to User so one key serves all
roles.
R14 and R15, notifications. A session produces an outcome notification for the student and for each professor,
and one reminder each the day before. Each notification concerns one session and has one recipient.
R16 and R17, cancellation and committee creation. A user may have cancelled many sessions, but a session
has at most one canceller. An administrator may have created many committees; every committee records its
creator.

5. Data Flow Design
5.1 Conventions
The design uses Gane-Sarson notation described in words. External entities are people or systems outside the
boundary and are numbered E1 to E4. Processes are numbered 1.0 to 9.0 and named with a verb. Data stores
are numbered D1 to D7 and correspond to ERD entities. Data flows carry named data between these elements. A
process never exchanges data directly with another process in this design; related processes communicate
through data stores. Level 0 shows the whole system as one process; Level 1 breaks it into nine processes.

6. Swimlane Design
6.1 Purpose and conventions
The swimlane (cross-functional) design describes who does what, in which order, across the whole defense
process. Each lane is one actor or system. A step belongs to exactly one lane. Steps are numbered by phase (for
example 5.3 is the third step of phase 5), and each phase maps to one DFD process, so the two views can be
checked against each other. Step types are: Start, Task, Decision (a question with branches), End (a final state)
and Optional (a step that may be skipped).

6.15 End-to-end narrative (main scenario)
• An administrator creates accounts and a committee. Professors and committee members log in and publish
single and recurring slots.

• A student logs in, opens the weekly grid, sees who is available and when, and optionally downloads the PDF.
• The student submits a request with an optional note. The administrator receives a notice.
• The administrator reviews the grid and assigns the request to a slot and date. Seats are created and the
professors are notified in the app.

• Each professor approves or rejects. When all approve, the session is confirmed and everyone receives a Bale
message; any rejection rejects it and frees the date.

8. API Contract
The API is shared by the web and Android clients. It is a JSON REST API under the prefix /api/v1. This section
defines its conventions and endpoints at the level needed by both client developers; field-level schemas are
derived from the attributes of section 4.

8.1 Conventions
Topic

Convention

Format

JSON request and response bodies, UTF-8. Dates are ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD); times are HH:MM in
Asia/Tehran; timestamps are ISO 8601 with offset.

Authentication

POST /auth/login returns an access token and a refresh token. Every other call (except the Bale webhook)
sends the access token in the Authorization header.

Authorization

The role in the token is checked on every call; a wrong role returns 403.

Errors

One format: status code plus a body with code, message and optional field errors. Codes include
VALIDATION_ERROR, UNAUTHENTICATED, FORBIDDEN, NOT_FOUND, CONFLICT (for example slot
occurrence taken) and RULE_VIOLATION (for example active request exists).

Versioning

The version is in the path. Breaking changes create /api/v2.

Pagination

List endpoints accept page and pageSize and return total.

Idempotency

Commands that create records accept an Idempotency-Key header so that a retried mobile request does not
create a duplicate.

Rate limiting

Login and link-code endpoints are limited per user and per address.

Enumerations

Sent as the lowercase literals of section 7.3.2.

The student opens My request, optionally writes a note and presses Submit. The page immediately shows the

9.5 Usability and accessibility principles
• Right-to-left layout for Persian and left-to-right for English, with the same components.
• Plain language for statuses: requested (waiting for assignment), assigned (waiting for professors), confirmed,
rejected, cancelled.

• Every error says what to do next, for example "This date is already taken, choose another date."
• Text size and contrast follow common accessibility guidance; all actions are available from the keyboard on the
web.

• Destructive actions (cancel, decline, withdraw) ask for confirmation.
• Forms keep the entered values after a validation error.

10. Security and Privacy
10.1 Authentication and sessions
Passwords are never stored in readable form; the system keeps a salted hash produced by a modern algorithm.
Login returns a short-lived access token and a longer-lived refresh token. Failed logins are rate limited, and
repeated failures lock the account for a short time. Deactivated accounts are rejected at login and their tokens are
refused on every call. Logout invalidates the refresh token.

10.4 Privacy
The system stores only data needed for scheduling: identity and role, student number, thesis title, availability,
request and decision history, messages and a Bale chat id. The chat id is used only for sending the two kinds of
notification and is never shown to other users. A user may unlink Bale at any time, after which the notification
status becomes skipped. Messages are visible only to the participants of the session. Deactivated accounts keep
their history for audit but cannot log in. Retention follows the university's rules; a default of keeping defense history
for several years and then archiving is suggested.

11. Bale Notification Integration
11.1 Why Bale and what changes from email
The supervisor asked for notifications to be delivered through a bot on the Bale messenger instead of email,
because students and professors read the messenger more reliably than email. The system therefore needs three
things: a way to know which Bale chat belongs to which user (linking), a way to send messages (the gateway), and
a record of what was sent (the notification log). All other notices remain inside the application.

Rule

Unlinked users

Must a user be required to link Bale before submitting or answering?

No; unlinked users are skipped.

Q5

Should the student see which professors are assigned before
confirmation?

Yes, after assignment.

Q6

How long must defense history be kept?

Several years, then archived.

Q7

Who may create committee slots: any member or only the administrator?

Any member (assumption A5).

Q8

Should the grid show already booked occurrences?

Not in this version; occupancy is checked at
assignment.

16.2 Possible future enhancements
• Suggestions to the administrator: the system proposes slots that fit a request and workload balance.
• Show booked occurrences on the grid and per-professor workload reports.
• Calendar export (iCalendar) for confirmed defenses.
• Room booking and room names on the schedule.
• Additional notification channels if the university requires them.
• Reassignment after rejection and an escalation rule for unanswered seats.
• Statistics for the department: number of defenses per period, average time from request to confirmation.

One student's defense request and its life cycle.

One professor's place in a session, with their own answer.

The statuses of a session.

The Saturday to Wednesday, 08:00 to 17:00 view generated from slots.
